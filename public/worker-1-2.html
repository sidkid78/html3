1. Ceasefire Tracks UNSCR_2735

Gaza Track (US-QATAR-EGYPT)

Baseline Framework follows a three-phase structure: six-week temporary ceasefire, partial exchange, and aid scaling, transitioning to permanent cessation and complete IDF withdrawal (Phase 2), followed by reconstruction (Phase 3).

Philadelphi & Rafah: Israel demands continued physical presence/surveillance. Egypt/Hamas reject, citing 1979 Camp David & sovereignty.

Netzarim Corridor: Friction over Israeli security screening for returning northern Gazans vs. Hamas demand for uninhibited movement.

Permanence: Hamas requires binding transition guarantees; Israel seeks operational right to resume combat if benchmarks fail.

Ratios: Disputes over Israeli vetoes on high-profile prisoners and exile conditions.

Lebanon Track UNSCR_1701

Mechanism centers on Hezbollah withdrawing heavy arms north of Litani, deploying 5,000–10k LAF alongside UNIFIL, overseen by a US-chaired five-party committee.

Vulnerabilities: Israeli operational freedom (backed by US letter), LAF financial constraints needing GCC funding, unresolved Blue Line disputes.

2. Regional Dynamics FRONT_LINE

Border Integrity & Airspace

  • Egypt: Prioritizes Sinai integrity, rejects forced displacement. Faces 50-60% Suez revenue drop. Coordinates intel but refuses IDF border presence legitimization.

IAMD & Maritime Security

CENTCOM CAOC telemetry and Link-16 sharing proved effective during Apr/Oct 2024 strikes. However, GCC limits offensive airspace use, retaining sovereign fire control.

Maritime: Minimal Arab participation in Operation Prosperity Guardian. States prioritize bilateral truces (Saudi-Houthi) or avoid domestic backlash over Red Sea shipping.

3. Security Architecture

Abraham Accords exist in a 'Cold Peace' (intel sharing remains, public normalization frozen). US-Saudi-Israel track is deadlocked; Saudi demands irreversible 1967-border Palestinian state pathway.

Key Findings

Gaza ceasefire talks remain deadlocked over permanent IDF presence along the Philadelphi and Netzarim corridors and the transition to a permanent cessation of hostilities.

CENTCOM's integrated air defense successfully coordinated radar cueing across Jordan/Gulf, but GCC states maintain strict prohibitions against offensive strike usage.

Arab participation in Red Sea maritime coalitions remains constrained; powers prioritized bilateral truces and domestic stability.

The Abraham Accords survive functionally as an intelligence baseline, but Saudi normalization remains barred without an irreversible path to Palestinian statehood.

Arab states refuse to provide funding or peacekeeping personnel for post-war Gaza without an empowered, reformed Palestinian Authority and a two-state framework.
