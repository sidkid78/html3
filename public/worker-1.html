Threat Assessment:
Iran and the Axis of Resistance

Key Findings

  • Hezbollah's rocket arsenal is degraded by ~80%, forcing a tactical pivot to drone attrition warfare and direct IRGC command oversight.
  • The Houthis have emerged as the most autonomous and kinetically viable proxy, commanding critical maritime chokepoints along the Bab al-Mandab Strait.
  • Iran has shifted its doctrine to 'sovereign deterrence,' normalizing direct preemptive missile and drone strikes against US and Israeli regional targets.
  • The loss of the Syrian land bridge has forced Iran to adopt gray-zone maritime smuggling routes and prioritize localized proxy manufacturing.
END OF SUMMARY_

1. Operational Status and Kinetic Output of Key Proxies

Lebanese Hezbollah (Northern Front): Significantly degraded but operationally active. Rocket stockpiles are reduced by ~80% to approximately 15,000 munitions. With senior leadership decimated, the IRGC has assumed direct command. Following IDF operations south of the Litani River, Hezbollah has transitioned to a war of attrition heavily reliant on suicide and FPV drones.

The Houthis (Ansar Allah, Yemen): The most autonomous and effective kinetic actor in the Axis. They maintain a maritime chokehold over the Bab al-Mandab Strait, recently captured the port city of Mokha, and execute long-range missile strikes against critical regional infrastructure (e.g., Abha Airport).

Islamic Resistance in Iraq (IRI): Maintaining high operational tempo targeting both US installations and regional energy assets (such as Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline). Iraqi factions are actively resisting state-led disarmament ahead of planned US Coalition withdrawals.

Hamas: Highly degraded operationally; conflict focus has largely migrated to northern, eastern, and southern regional vectors.

2. Iranian Escalations, Strike Capabilities, and Deterrence Thresholds

Strategic Doctrine: Under Mojtaba Khamenei, Iran has abandoned "strategic patience" for "sovereign deterrence," operating on a total-war footing using decentralized subterranean missile/drone complexes.

Kinetic Escalations: Direct kinetic exchanges have normalized, marked by preemptive missile and drone strikes against US military facilities across the Persian Gulf (e.g., Kuwait, UAE).

Escalation Tripwires:

  • Lebanon: Permanent IDF buffer zones up to or beyond the Litani River trigger direct ballistic missile barrages against Israeli population centers.
  • Red Sea: Houthi attempts to formally tax shipping or annex strategic maritime islands prompt major coalition naval retaliation.
  • Iraq: Aggressive attempts by the central Iraqi government to disarm Popular Mobilization Forces trigger multi-front insurgencies against state assets and Gulf energy hubs.

3. Supply Lines, Weapons Transfers, and Regional Command Structure

Command and Control (C2): Decentralized franchise model. IRGC exercises direct oversight in Lebanon due to leadership losses, while Houthis operate with near-total autonomy.

Supply Lines: The collapse of the Syrian Assad regime severed the traditional overland "Land Bridge." Weapons transfers have shifted to maritime gray-zone logistics (e.g., feeder routes via Djibouti), commercial overland smuggling, and localized proxy manufacturing.

Financial Architecture: Following international bans on Hezbollah's al-Qard al-Hassan network, funding relies on decentralized black-market nodes, hawala networks, and cryptocurrency rails.
